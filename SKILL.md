# Namer

A naming workflow for software products, mobile applications, developer tools, and consumer brands.

Great names carry compressed meaning. They plant concrete images that make sense quickly and hold up over time.

## Core rules

1. Avoid predictable tech affixes. Skip prefixes like Smart, Auto, Hyper, or Insta, and suffixes like -ly, -ify, -io, -hub, -flow, and -ai.
2. Respect the bar test and phone test. If someone says the name in a noisy room or spells it on a phone call, the listener should write it down correctly without asking twice.
3. Aim for suggestive and coined terms over purely descriptive ones. Descriptive names face harder trademark registration hurdles and blend into search results. Suggestive and coined names provide stronger brand protection.
4. Verify cross-language phonetics and false cognates. Check how the name sounds and whether it carries unintended meanings in the product's main release languages, particularly English, Portuguese, Spanish, and Chinese.
5. Check real availability with tools before presenting candidates. A name that sounds great but belongs to a direct competitor or established trademark in the same market class is not a candidate.

## Taxonomy of naming types

- Metaphorical: Draws on physical analogies, natural elements, or conceptual tools (Compass, Nest, Figma, Discord). Suited for consumer products, lifestyle apps, and social platforms.
- Coined: Newly minted words designed for distinct sound and trademark strength (Spotify, Miro, Kodak, Hulu). Suited for companies that need an uncontested legal mark.
- Compound: Pairs two concrete concepts cleanly without feeling forced (Snapchat, Pinterest, Airtable, Raycast). Suited for workflow tools and visual software.
- Action: Uses an active verb or command (Slack, Chase, Loom, Zoom, Run). Suited for productivity and daily routine tools.
- Proprietary short: One or two syllables with crisp consonants (Linear, Vercel, Notion, Uber). Suited for modern utilities and developer platforms.
- Subtle descriptive: Hints at the function without using generic catalog terms (Basecamp, Pocket, App Store). Suited for straightforward utilities.

## The seven-step naming workflow

### 1. Gather the brief
Collect or establish:
- Core function in one sentence.
- Primary audience and use cases.
- Tone and character (editorial, warm, technical, playful, understated).
- Constraints, off-limit terms, and names of close competitors.
- Target languages and release markets.
- Essential platforms (Apple App Store, Google Play, web domain, package registries, social handles).

### 2. Map metaphor territories
Explore conceptual anchors before generating candidate words:
- What physical object or process resembles this product?
- What transformation or feeling does the user experience?
- Which textures, materials, or gestures connect to the workflow?
Pick two or three clear territories to explore.

### 3. Generate candidate names
Generate 30 to 50 raw candidates across the chosen territories. Mix single words, compounds, modified forms, and sound-first combinations. Keep this step internal.

### 4. Apply anti-pattern filters
Drop names that show these weaknesses:
- Sounds like generic software generated by an algorithm.
- Relies on awkward silent letters or unusual punctuation.
- Fails the phone test or produces cacophony when spoken aloud.
- Sounds unnatural in common sentences ("download the app", "save it to...", "share your...").

### 5. Verify availability
Check candidates against actual platforms before showing them:
- Search Apple App Store and Google Play for apps with identical or overlapping features.
- Check trademark databases and established retail brands in the same category.
- Verify DNS and domain records for patterns such as [name].app, use[name].com, or get[name].com.
- Check relevant social handles and package registries if applicable.
Drop names with direct conflicts. Keep alternatives only when a clear workaround exists.

### 6. Evaluate and compare
Score the survivors on memorability, ease of pronunciation across target languages, fit with the desired tone, and legal defensibility.

### 7. Present finalists
Present three to five vetted finalists. For each one, provide:
- The name and its naming type.
- Origin story in two sentences.
- Why it works phonetically and semantically.
- Natural sentence test in the target language.
- Verified availability status across stores and domains.
- Any known trade-offs.
- Suggested positioning tagline.

Let finalists sit for 24 hours before making a final decision.
